"Free email signature generator" is one of the most frequent searches in this field, and the honest
answer is nuanced: yes, you can create a complete professional signature without paying. No, that
does not solve the problem of a hundred-person company. Here is exactly where the line runs.

## What a free generator genuinely allows

Creating a quality signature for yourself should cost nothing, and effectively costs nothing. You
can:

- start from a template and adapt it to your colours;
- place your logo, correctly sized;
- structure name, job title and contact details with a readable hierarchy;
- add two or three social icons;
- preview the rendering on the main clients;
- retrieve the HTML and install it in Outlook or Gmail.

For a freelancer, a small business, or simply to equip yourself with a decent signature before
convincing management, that is amply sufficient. It is what
[the Signally editor](/en/features) offers, without a credit card.

## The two traps of free generators

**Inserted advertising.** Some tools add a discreet line — "Signature created with X" — at the bottom
of your block. It travels to all your recipients, on every one of your commercial exchanges. It is
the tool's business model, and you are the medium. Check this before adopting anything.

**Logo hosting.** Your logo has to live on a public URL. If the generator hosts it, the durability of
your signature depends on theirs: a service that closes, a URL migration, and the image breaks in
every signature — including in messages already sent and archived by your customers. The technical
constraints are detailed in
[adding a logo without breaking it](/en/blog/outlook-signature-logo).

## Where free really stops

The limit is not in the quality of the signature produced. It is in **what happens afterwards**.

A generator gives you an HTML block. It cannot:

- install it on fifty mailboxes without fifty people doing the procedure;
- automatically fill each person's name and job title from your directory;
- stop an employee changing the logo or deleting the legal notices;
- update every signature at once at the next brand change;
- remove the signature of someone leaving the company;
- schedule a banner from 1 to 30 March on the sales teams only.

None of those functions makes sense for a single user. All of them become structural as soon as there
is an estate.

## Working out the tipping point

The reasoning is mechanical. Take your headcount, the number of signature changes per year, and the
time actually spent per signature — creation, sending the instructions, chasing, checking.

A 200-person organisation, with 4 changes a year and 12 minutes per signature, ties up 160 hours
annually. At the loaded hourly cost of an administrative role, that comfortably exceeds the price of
a subscription. The [cost calculator](/en/pricing) runs that calculation with your own assumptions,
and the article
[how much manual management costs](/en/blog/cost-of-managing-email-signatures) details the method.

In practice, the tipping point sits between twenty and thirty mailboxes. It arrives earlier if legal
notice compliance is a subject for your legal department, or if you have several brands.

> **Worth knowing:** the right order is almost always the same. You start free, you create a
> signature you are happy with, you show it internally — and it is that signature that becomes the
> argument for funding the rollout. Nobody funds a project on a mockup.

## How to assess a generator before committing to it

Four questions, asked before rather than after.

**Is the HTML exportable?** You have to be able to take your code and leave. A tool that only gives
access to a hosted link locks you in.

**Is the rendering checked on Outlook for Windows?** It is the most demanding client, with its
Word-inherited rendering engine. A generator that does not preview it will produce surprises.

**Is the logo hosted stably?** See above.

**Is there continuity towards deployment?** If the free generator and the enterprise tool are two
unrelated products, you will redo everything when you scale up. If it is the same vendor, the
template created for free becomes the company template directly — that is the case here.
