This grid is made to be used as is. Send the eight questions to each shortlisted vendor, ask for
written answers, and compare the answers with each other. We give our own answers for each criterion;
ask for theirs.

> Product characteristics change. This grid does not claim to describe any particular vendor's offer:
> it provides the questions, to be asked directly, at the date of your project.

## Criterion 1 — Deployment method

**The question:** is the signature inserted client-side while writing, or server-side after sending?
Does the message travel through the vendor's infrastructure?

**Why it is decisive:** client-side insertion makes the signature visible to the sender and in sent
items, avoids stacking in threads, and does not touch routing. Server-side insertion has the opposite
properties. It is the criterion that determines both the daily user experience and the extent of the
security review.

**Our answer:** Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace add-ins, insertion while writing, with no rerouting
and no connector.

## Criterion 2 — Data location

**The question:** where are the synchronised directory and the templates hosted? Which jurisdiction
does the vendor company fall under? Which sub-processors are involved?

**Why it is decisive:** location and jurisdiction are two distinct questions, often conflated. The full
reasoning is in [data sovereignty](/en/blog/eu-data-residency-saas).

**Our answer:** hosting in France, European vendor, no transfer outside the European Union as part of
the service.

## Criterion 3 — Access to email content

**The question:** does the supplier access message content at any point? What exact permissions are
requested at admin consent?

**Why it is decisive:** it is your security officer's first question, and it can be verified
objectively on the Microsoft or Google consent screen — see
[can a signature tool read your email](/en/blog/email-signature-tool-security).

**Our answer:** no email content read, analysed, stored or routed through us. Only the directory
attributes displayed in the signature are synchronised, read-only.

## Criterion 4 — Scheduled campaigns

**The question:** are dated banners, per-team targeting and automatic succession included, or reserved
for a higher edition?

**Why it is decisive:** it is the function that turns a cost centre into a communication channel. It is
frequently positioned as a paid option, which changes the budget calculation — see
[email signature marketing](/en/blog/email-signature-marketing).

**Our answer:** included in the single price, with no higher edition.

## Criterion 5 — The price list

**The question:** is the price public? Is there a minimum commitment? How is a user counted — are
shared mailboxes and aliases billed?

**Why it is decisive:** a public price lets you position yourself before any sales discussion. The
counting method can move the invoice by 20% at identical headcount.

**Our answer:** public grid from €1.50 down to €0.50 excl. VAT per user per month, decreasing with
volume, available on the [pricing](/en/pricing) page. Shared mailboxes and aliases not billed
separately.

## Criterion 6 — A real trial

**The question:** can you create a signature and test it before talking to a salesperson?

**Why it is decisive:** a tool that only opens its product after a demo prevents you forming an
independent view. It is also a signal about the vendor's confidence in their own product.

**Our answer:** editor freely available at app.signally.io, with no credit card.

## Criterion 7 — Support and language

**The question:** is the team reachable in your time zone and your language? Is support included or
billed?

**Why it is decisive:** initial deployment is when you need help most, and a nine-hour time difference
turns a ten-minute question into a two-day wait.

**Our answer:** European team, support included in the subscription.

## Criterion 8 — Reversibility

**The question:** do you get your templates and artwork back if you leave? Within what timeframe is the
data deleted? Is an export possible without involving support?

**Why it is decisive:** it is the most frequently forgotten criterion, because it only comes up when it
is too late. It belongs among the elements written into the processing agreement — see
[email signatures and the GDPR](/en/blog/email-signature-gdpr).

**Our answer:** export of templates and artwork, complete deletion of the workspace on request.

## How to use the answers

Do not weight the eight criteria uniformly: two or three genuinely matter in your context, and you know
which.

An IT department in a regulated sector will weight criteria 2, 3 and 8 heavily. A marketing director
will look at 4 and 6 first. A finance director will compare 5 with the current cost of manual
management, calculable in the [calculator](/en/pricing).

> **Worth knowing:** a question left without a written answer is an answer. Record it as such in your
> table rather than leaving it blank: it is often the most useful information in the exercise.
