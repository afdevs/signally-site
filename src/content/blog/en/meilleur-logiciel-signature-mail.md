Searching for "the best email signature software" assumes there is one. In reality, the market splits
into three families answering three distinct needs, and the first job is to identify yours. Comparing
an individual generator with a fleet manager makes no more sense than comparing a word processor with
a document management system.

> This article describes families of tools and an evaluation method. It does not rank named vendors:
> product characteristics change, and only direct verification with each of them, at the date of your
> project, carries any value.

## Family 1 — The individual generator

An online editor that produces an HTML block you install yourself in your email client.

**What it does well:** quickly produce a decent signature, often free of charge. Useful for a
freelancer, a very small outfit, or to equip yourself with a mockup before taking a project internal.

**Its limit:** it stops at producing the block. No deployment, no synchronisation, no locking. Each
person has to do the procedure, and nothing prevents divergence afterwards.

The subject is covered in
[free email signature generators](/en/blog/free-email-signature-generator).

## Family 2 — The fleet manager

A tool that applies the signature across the organisation, drawing on your directory.

**What it brings:** automatic application with no action from the employee, fields filled from the
directory, granular locking, per-team templates, banner campaigns, statistics.

**Its condition:** it assumes a correctly filled directory and an initial deployment by IT.

That is the family matching most organisations beyond twenty or so mailboxes. Signally sits there.

**The point of differentiation within that family** is deployment architecture — client-side add-in or
server-side relay — developed below.

## Family 3 — The platforms' native functions

Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace each offer a server-side footer mechanism.

**What they do well:** force a legal notice onto 100% of outbound traffic, including application sends
that never pass through an email client. No third-party tool does that.

**Their limits:** no per-employee custom field, minimal formatting, stacking in threads, a signature
invisible to the sender, no campaign scheduling.

The detail is in
[add-in or Exchange transport rule](/en/blog/outlook-add-in-vs-exchange-transport-rule) and
[Google Workspace footers](/en/blog/google-workspace-email-signature).

## The most structuring criterion: architecture

Before price, before features, one question splits the tools into two groups: **where is the signature
inserted?**

**Client-side, while writing.** The add-in writes into the current message. The sender sees their
signature, it appears in sent items, the message leaves your tenant with no detour.

**Server-side, after sending.** The message is modified in transit. The sender sees nothing, threads
stack blocks, and depending on the implementation, the message may travel through a third party's
infrastructure.

That difference is not an implementation detail: it determines what your security officer will have to
validate and what your users will experience daily. It is analysed in
[can a signature tool read your email](/en/blog/email-signature-tool-security).

## The evaluation method

It comes in three stages, and it beats a comparative reading of product pages.

**1. Qualify your family of need.** Headcount, number of legal entities, need for campaigns, compliance
requirements. That generally eliminates two families out of three.

**2. Apply an identical grid to each candidate.** The same eight questions, put in writing, to the two
or three vendors retained. The grid is in
[choosing your tool: the eight criteria](/en/blog/email-signature-software-comparison).

**3. Test for real.** A sales demo shows what the vendor wants to show. A trial on a pilot group of ten
people, over a week, shows what you will live with. Take the opportunity to check rendering on Outlook
for Windows, behaviour on replies, and display on mobile.

> **Worth knowing:** systematically ask for written answers on data location, access scope and
> reversibility. A vendor who answers readily out loud and drags their feet in writing is telling you
> what their support will be worth.

## The economic calculation

It does not come down to the headline price. The useful comparison sets the subscription cost against
the cost of the internal time currently spent on manual management — the method is detailed in
[what manual management costs](/en/blog/cost-of-managing-email-signatures), and the direct calculation
is in the [calculator](/en/pricing).

Two things to watch on price lists: check whether banner campaigns are included or reserved for a
higher edition, and whether the price is public or quoted on request. Pricing transparency changes the
nature of the negotiation, and it is in fact one of our eight criteria.
