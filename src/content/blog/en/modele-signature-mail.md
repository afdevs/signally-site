Searching for an "email signature template" usually lands you in a gallery of images with no
instructions. Yet choosing a template is not primarily an aesthetic matter: it depends on what your
profession expects from an email. A notary and a SaaS salesperson do not have the same display
priorities.

## The three base structures

Before the sectors, the shapes. Three structures cover most needs.

**Vertical.** Everything left-aligned, logo above or below the text block. It is the most robust: it
falls apart on no client, no screen. Prefer it when in doubt.

**Horizontal.** Logo on the left, information block on the right, separated by a vertical rule in the
accent colour. More elegant, it requires at least 480 px of width and shrinks badly below that.

**Compact.** Three lines — name, job title, phone — for replies in a thread. It avoids stacking full
blocks in a ten-exchange conversation.

Layouts with three or more columns should be ruled out: they systematically break on mobile.

## Twelve variations by sector

**1. Consulting and professional services.** Precise job title, one direct number, a booking link. A
photo is defensible here: the relationship is long and personal.

**2. Property.** Mobile number prominent, professional licence and mandate number depending on your
status, a link to current listings. It is a profession where people call back: the phone comes first.

**3. Healthcare and regulated professions.** Exact title, professional registration number where
applicable, practice contact details. Total sobriety: no banner, no social links, no strapline.

**4. Law firm.** Bar association, legal form, mandatory notices. The confidentiality disclaimer has
real meaning here, unlike in many other sectors — see
[confidentiality disclaimers](/en/blog/email-confidentiality-disclaimer).

**5. Industry and technical B2B.** Site, switchboard and direct extension, certifications if
genuinely held. The logo matters more than social links.

**6. Agency and creative.** This is the sector where a more graphic signature is allowed: a confident
accent colour, a link to the portfolio. Take care to stay within rendering constraints.

**7. Software vendor.** A link to the documentation or the support portal for technical teams, a demo
link for salespeople. Two distinct templates are justified.

**8. Retail and distribution.** Store address, opening hours if relevant, a link to the customer
review for after-sales.

**9. Non-profit and public sector.** The organisation's identity before the person's, funding notices
if required.

**10. Recruitment.** A link to current openings, the person's professional profile. It is a
profession where the campaign banner pays off particularly well — see
[campaigns and banners](/en/campaigns).

**11. Finance and insurance.** Professional registration, supervisory authority, regulatory warnings.
Sobriety and completeness.

**12. Multi-brand group.** One template per entity, same structural style, logo and notices specific
to each subsidiary. The mechanism is described in
[multi-brand signatures](/en/blog/multi-brand-email-signatures).

## What every successful template has in common

Whatever the sector, the same constraints apply: a maximum width of 500 to 600 px, text as text
rather than as an image, a system font, one accent colour, a hierarchy with three size levels. Those
rules are detailed in
[how to create a professional email signature](/en/blog/create-professional-email-signature).

A template that violates those constraints will look good in the gallery and broken in your
customer's inbox.

> **Worth knowing:** be wary of templates offered as an image to copy. What circulates as
> "inspiration" is often a screenshot of an idealised rendering, never tested on Outlook for Windows.

## A template is only useful once it becomes a managed model

The trap of a template is that it produces an individual block. You adapt it to your name, you send
it to your colleagues, each adapts it to theirs — and you end up with forty diverging variants in
three weeks.

A template only has lasting value once converted into a **managed model**: personal information is
replaced by dynamic fields filled from the directory, the structure and the logo are locked, and
assignment happens per team.
