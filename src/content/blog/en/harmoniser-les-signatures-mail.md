Standardising an organisation's signatures is a project whose difficulty almost never lies in the
technology. Deploying an add-in takes a quarter of an hour, across fifty mailboxes or a thousand.
What changes with size is change management.

## Step 1 — Measure the real gap

Do not start from an exhaustive audit: it is expensive and teaches nothing a sample does not.

Ask twenty or so people, spread across departments and levels, to forward you a message they sent
this week. Count: how many different layouts, how many versions of the logo, how many legal notices
present, how many expired job titles.

That picture is your best budget argument. A decision-maker who sees six contradictory signatures
from their own company no longer needs convincing.

## Step 2 — Settle the locking before designing

This is the structuring decision, and it is political more than graphic. Field by field: what is
locked, what stays free?

Our recommendation, which works in the vast majority of cases: lock the logo, the structure, the
colours and the legal notices. Leave the direct phone number free and, optionally, a personal link —
a booking page, a professional profile.

That tiny margin changes everything in acceptance. A total constraint triggers ingenious workarounds;
a constraint with a space of one's own passes without debate.

## Step 3 — Clean the directory

This is the systematically underestimated task, and it conditions the result: what is missing in the
directory will be missing in the signatures, and what is inconsistent there will become public.

Concentrate on four attributes: job title, department, phone, site. Normalise the titles — "Sales
Mgr" and "Sales Manager" have to converge — and move the numbers to the international format.

Check too that your template makes empty lines disappear rather than showing an orphaned label. The
detail is in
[synchronising signatures with the directory](/en/blog/entra-id-email-signature-sync).

## Step 4 — The pilot

Ten or so people, chosen for their diversity rather than their goodwill: someone from sales, someone
from support, someone who writes mostly from their phone, someone from a subsidiary if you have one,
and if possible someone known to be demanding.

One week, with three specific checks rather than a general opinion:

1. Does the signature appear on a new message **and** on a reply?
2. Are the personal details accurate?
3. Is the rendering clean for an external recipient? Have them send a message to a personal address.

## Step 5 — Go wide, at a pace that depends on size

This is where size changes the method.

**Up to 50 mailboxes.** A single switch, an announcement message, and it is done. The volume of
questions stays absorbable by one person.

**From 50 to 200 mailboxes.** Two or three waves, by department. Plan a named contact per wave, to
whom questions go, rather than a generic support channel.

**Beyond 200 mailboxes.** Proceed by organisational unit, a day or two apart, starting with the
simplest structures. You will never have more than a handful of tickets at once, and each wave
benefits from the lessons of the last.

**Beyond 1,000 mailboxes.** Same logic, but coordination with local entities becomes the real
subject: each subsidiary has its contact, its legal notices and sometimes its language — see
[multi-brand signatures](/en/blog/multi-brand-email-signatures).

> **Worth knowing:** every wave has to come with the instruction to clear the old local signature,
> **with a screenshot**. Without that, the two blocks stack up. It is the number one incident in this
> kind of project, and it is trivial to avoid.

## Step 6 — Lock in the gains

Once standardisation is done, two mechanisms prevent drift.

**Life cycle automation.** Arrivals, moves and departures have to be reflected with no intervention.
If every change requires a manual action, the gap returns within months.

**A short policy.** Two pages, to settle what the tool does not: photos, personal social accounts,
executive exceptions. The model is in
[email signature policy](/en/blog/email-signature-policy).

## What the project delivers, beyond consistency

The visible benefit is graphic. The lasting benefits are elsewhere: the end of signature tickets for
IT, the certainty that legal notices are present everywhere, and the opening of a usable
communication channel with [scheduled banners](/en/campaigns).

The full economic calculation — internal time against subscription cost — is detailed in
[what manual management costs](/en/blog/cost-of-managing-email-signatures).
