An available, free channel stays unused as long as nobody has an idea of what to put in it. Here are
twelve proven campaigns spread across the year, each with the audience to target and the action to
offer.

## First quarter

**1. New year outlook.** The whole company, two to three weeks in January. A sober banner, focused on
what you are preparing rather than on the greetings themselves. Action: see the roadmap, or nothing at
all — this is one of the rare campaigns where an absence of clicks is acceptable.

**2. Trade show or industry event.** Sales teams and management, three weeks before the event, stopping
the evening it closes. Action: book a meeting at the stand. It is the most profitable use of the
channel, because the message lands precisely with the people you are going to see.

**3. Annual publication.** The whole company, over a month. Activity report, sector study, barometer.
Action: download. It also generates contacts if the download goes through a form.

## Second quarter

**4. Product launch or new offer.** Sales and marketing first, then wider. Action: see what is new.
Take care not to let it run for six months: something new that lasts is no longer new.

**5. Hiring campaign.** The whole company, continuously during heavy recruitment periods. Action: see
openings. Your employees become your first sourcing channel, in front of an audience that knows them
professionally — that is the HR use case described on the [use cases](/en/use-cases) page.

**6. Webinar or online event.** The teams in contact with the target, two weeks ahead. Action: sign up.
The short lead time matters: a webinar announced six weeks in advance generates forgotten
registrations.

## Third quarter

**7. Certification or accreditation earned.** The whole company, one month. ISO, industry
accreditation, verifiable CSR commitment. Action: find out more. This campaign works on credibility
rather than conversion.

**8. Satisfaction survey.** Support and customer service, for the duration of the survey. Action:
respond. The response rate is markedly higher than for a dedicated email, because the request arrives
after a real exchange.

**9. Summer background campaign.** The whole company, the quiet period. A low-urgency message that
deserves to exist: your positioning, a reference page, a pillar piece of content.

## Fourth quarter

**10. Commercial peak.** Sales, over the relevant period. End-of-year offer, renewal terms. It is the
most explicitly promotional campaign in the calendar: keep it short.

**11. Review and thanks.** The whole company, December. The year's figures, thanks to customers. A
relational register, not a commercial one.

**12. Organisational announcement.** Ad hoc, as news requires. A move, a new subsidiary, a change of
company name. Action: read the announcement. This campaign has real practical use — it informs
correspondents you would not have thought to notify.

## The two permanent campaigns that pay off most

Alongside the calendar, two banners deserve to run continuously on specific teams. They are not
spectacular, and they are generally the most profitable.

**The booking link**, under the sales team's signature. Every exchange becomes an opportunity to fix a
slot, with no extra follow-up.

**The review request**, under support's signature. It arrives at the right moment: just after a
problem is solved, when satisfaction is at its peak.

Those two cost nothing to produce and work all year.

> **Worth knowing:** never broadcast two simultaneous campaigns to the same team. If two messages are
> urgent at once, choose, or postpone. A rotation dilutes both.

## Building your own calendar

The list above is a starting point, not a plan. The right calendar starts from your real deadlines:
your trade shows, your launches, your recruitment periods.
